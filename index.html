Overview

I reframed three related Kalik product efforts into one enterprise SaaS application for regulated artwork operations. The platform supports the complete lifecycle: create artwork, manage files and approvals, validate labels with AI, and release production-ready assets with a clear audit trail.

Problem

Artwork teams were working across disconnected tools. Designers struggled with a legacy editor. Reviewers lost track of the latest file in email threads. Compliance teams manually proofread labels and missed critical errors. The result was slow approvals, version conflicts, inconsistent user experiences, and expensive print or regulatory risk.

Users
  • Factory and operations users needed a simple way to make controlled edits without learning a professional design tool.
  • Brand and artwork designers needed precision controls, version history, file locking, and predictable collaboration.
  • Regulatory reviewers needed AI support for copy, barcode, artwork, and regional compliance checks before release.
  • Managers and approvers needed status visibility, audit trails, and fewer email-led handoffs.
Research Insight

Across interviews, usability sessions, and workflow mapping, the pattern was clear: the same artwork object moved through different mental models. Creation needed flexibility, approval needed control, and validation needed confidence. Treating them as separate tools made the journey brittle. The design needed one shared artwork backbone with role-specific surfaces.

Platform Architecture
CreateSimple and Pro editor modes support both beginners and expert designers.
ManageSmart file locks, version timeline, comments, and status states protect the source of truth.
ApproveStructured review flows replace scattered email approvals and make blockers visible.
ValidateNLP and computer vision checks catch label copy, artwork, and compliance issues inline.
ReleaseFinal files move to print handoff with confidence, history, and accountability.
GovernA shared design system keeps six product areas visually and behaviorally consistent.
Key Design Decisions
  • Mode Switching - Simple mode reduced fear for occasional users, while Pro mode kept advanced controls for expert designers.
  • Smart File Locking - Ownership, wait states, and estimated unlock timing prevented conflicting edits without hiding collaboration context.
  • Inline AI Validation - High-confidence issues appeared on the canvas with rule references, suggested fixes, and override controls.
  • Status-Led Navigation - Draft, review, approved, and rejected states became navigational anchors rather than buried metadata.
  • Design System Governance - Shared patterns for comments, approvals, alerts, and validation scaled across the suite instead of being redesigned in each product.
Testing & Iteration

Testing focused on practical task completion: editing artwork, finding the latest file, resolving approval blockers, interpreting AI validation results, and handing off final labels. Iterations simplified novice actions, strengthened file-status visibility, and made AI confidence easier to understand without asking users to trust a black box.

The Challenge

A UK social housing association managing 50,000+ units across 20,000 sites had no unified digital platform. Four distinct operational roles — Director of Assets, Void Manager, Compliance Officer, and Repairs Manager — each relied on separate spreadsheets, email chains, and legacy tools to track KPIs, compliance obligations, and repair workflows. Data was siloed, decisions were delayed, and costly compliance failures were going undetected until they became regulatory incidents.

The brief was ambitious: design a single platform that could surface the right information to the right person at the right time — without forcing every role into the same one-size-fits-all view.

My Role & Approach

I led discovery, information architecture, UX design, and prototyping across all four role tracks in parallel. I facilitated workshops with Housing Directors, Void Managers, Compliance teams, and Field Repair staff to map their distinct mental models, decision triggers, and data needs. The key insight: each role defined "urgent" differently. A compliance officer responds to certificate expiry. A void manager responds to re-let timeline slippage. A repairs manager responds to SLA breach risk. One dashboard could not serve all four.

The 4 Role Dashboards
DIRECTOR OF ASSETS
Portfolio-wide KPIs. Decent Homes Standard score (target 95%). Overdue compliance certs. Cost analysis vs estimated. Stock condition surveys. Program builder for capital works.
VOID MANAGER

COMPLIANCE OFFICER
Gas safety, EPC, fire risk, and electrical certificates by status. Overdue count with escalation flags. Upcoming expiry calendar. Regulatory risk score per property block.
REPAIRS MANAGER
Active cases by type: Disrepair, Damp & Mould, Major. Work order queue with SLA status. Contractor assignment. Component replacement tracking. Repairs cost vs budget.

Key Design Decisions
  • Role-Adaptive Entry Points — Login detects user role and routes to their specialised dashboard. No configuration required. Each view surfaces only the alerts, metrics, and actions relevant to that role.
  • Smart Alert System — System-flagged data issues and inspection triggers appear as contextual banners with inline "Review" CTAs, not buried in notification panels.
  • Progress vs Target Design — Every KPI shows current value alongside target threshold (e.g. 30-day re-let target, £1200 cost-per-void standard) so users can self-assess performance without digging into reports.
  • Audit Trail & Activity Feed — A time-stamped activity sidebar gives all roles visibility of recent actions (surveys completed, certificates issued, void status changes) to replace manual status emails.
  • Responsive Hierarchy — Sites → Block → Core → Units drill-down model with 20K sites and 50K units required a spatial hierarchy navigation pattern, not flat search.

Overview

I designed an end-to-end workplace visitor experience system that turns check-in into a connected service moment. One check-in triggers access control, host notifications, room readiness, navigation guidance, hospitality preparation, and visitor support.

Problem

Clients experienced delays and confusion during check-in. Hosts lacked reliable tools to plan visits and arrange hospitality. Receptionists had to handle access, coordination, questions, and peak visitor traffic manually, which made premium visits feel fragmented.

Users
  • Visitor / Client needs fast entry, clear guidance, and a premium hospitality experience.
  • Host / Employee needs visit planning, tracking, room readiness, and coordination visibility.
  • Receptionist needs reduced manual workload and better prioritization during peak times.
Research & Insights

Discovery included receptionist interviews, host discussions, and feedback from high-value clients. The strongest insight was that check-in was not a single kiosk interaction; it was a chain of dependent moments across people, access, space, and hospitality.

  • Reception became overloaded during visitor peaks.
  • Hosts often forgot food, room, or access arrangements.
  • Clients felt lost after check-in because the system stopped helping too early.
  • Manual coordination created delays and inconsistent service quality.
Requirements
  • Improve client experience from arrival through exit.
  • Reduce receptionist workload through self-service and automation.
  • Enable hosts to pre-register visitors, request access, book rooms, and arrange hospitality.
  • Give visitors quick check-in, automatic access, clear navigation, and reliable support.
End-to-End Flow
Pre-VisitHost schedules visit, registers client, requests access, and books room or hospitality.
AI Check-inVisitor self-checks in using face recognition, phone number, or QR code.
Auto AccessSystem activates approved access and notifies the host and reception.
GuidanceVisitor receives meeting room, floor, WiFi, and directional support.
HospitalityRefreshments and room readiness are triggered at the right time.
ExitCheckout code closes the visit and revokes access.
Key Features
  • AI-enabled self check-in through face recognition, mobile number, or QR code.
  • Automated access control connected to visitor registration and checkout.
  • Real-time notifications for hosts, reception, access, and hospitality teams.
  • Visitor assistant for directions, meeting-room help, WiFi support, host connection, and refreshment requests.
  • Host dashboard for visit planning, today's visitors, meeting preparation, and quick actions.
Testing & Iteration

I validated the experience through task-based usability testing: complete check-in, find the meeting room, and request assistance. Early users were unsure what happened after access was approved, so the design added stronger step indicators, confirmation states, and post-check-in guidance.
